Group and summarise data with groupby

Split a table into groups, summarise each group (sum, mean, count) and put the answers back together.

1What is it?

groupby() splits a DataFrame into groups that share a value in some column, calculates a summary for each group, and combines the results into one small table.

2Why do we need it?

Most business questions are about groups: total sales per city, average marks per section, orders per month. Writing this by hand needs loops and dictionaries. groupby does it in one line.

3In plain words

Everyday example

Imagine a pile of shop bills. You sort them into one tray per city, then add up each tray. Splitting into trays is the group step, and adding is the summary step.

Precise definition

groupby follows a split-apply-combine pattern. df.groupby("city")["amount"].sum() splits by city, applies sum to amount inside each group and combines the results into a Series indexed by city. agg() applies several summaries at once.

  1. Choose the column to group by ("city").
  2. Choose the column to summarise ("amount").
  3. Choose the summary: sum, mean, count, max, min.
  4. Optionally use agg() for several summaries and sort_values() to rank them.
  5. Use reset_index() if you want the group labels back as a normal column.

4Code, step by step

Quick summaries of one column

lesson_1.py
import pandas as pd

sales = pd.DataFrame({
    "city": ["Pune", "Delhi", "Pune", "Delhi", "Mumbai", "Pune"],
    "item": ["pen", "pen", "bag", "bag", "pen", "pen"],
    "amount": [200, 150, 900, 700, 300, 250],
})
print("Total:", sales["amount"].sum())
print("Average:", sales["amount"].mean())
print("Largest:", sales["amount"].max())
best = sales["amount"].idxmax()
print("Best row:", best, "->", sales.loc[best, "city"])
OUTPUT
Total: 2500
Average: 416.6666666666667
Largest: 900
Best row: 2 -> Pune
LineWhat it means
sales["amount"].sum()Adds up the whole column. mean, max, min and count work the same way.
sales["amount"].idxmax()Returns the label of the row with the largest value, not the value itself.
sales.loc[best, "city"]Uses that label to look up the city on the best row.

import pandas as pd

Several summaries at once

lesson_3.py
import pandas as pd

sales = pd.DataFrame({
    "city": ["Pune", "Delhi", "Pune", "Delhi", "Mumbai", "Pune"],
    "amount": [200, 150, 900, 700, 300, 250],
})
print(sales.groupby("city")["amount"].agg(["sum", "mean", "count"]))

report = sales.groupby("city").agg(total=("amount", "sum"), orders=("amount", "count"))
print(report.sort_values("total", ascending=False))
OUTPUT
         sum   mean  count
city                      
Delhi    850  425.0      2
Mumbai   300  300.0      1
Pune    1350  450.0      3
        total  orders
city                 
Pune     1350       3
Delhi     850       2
Mumbai    300       1
LineWhat it means
.agg(["sum", "mean", "count"])Runs all three summaries and gives one column for each.
agg(total=("amount", "sum"), orders=("amount", "count"))Named aggregation: new_name=(column, summary). It lets you choose the column names of the result.
sort_values("total", ascending=False)Ranks the cities from the highest total to the lowest.

Group by two columns

lesson_4.py
import pandas as pd

sales = pd.DataFrame({
    "city": ["Pune", "Delhi", "Pune", "Delhi", "Mumbai", "Pune"],
    "item": ["pen", "pen", "bag", "bag", "pen", "pen"],
    "amount": [200, 150, 900, 700, 300, 250],
})
result = sales.groupby(["city", "item"])["amount"].sum()
print(result)
print(result.reset_index())
OUTPUT

groupby(["city", "item"])A list groups by the combination, such as Pune+pen and Pune+bag.
result.reset_index()Turns the group labels back into normal columns, giving a flat table that is easy to save or plot.

5Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the summary step. df.groupby("city") alone only creates a grouping object and calculates nothing.
  • Expecting the group labels to be a normal column. They become the index unless you call reset_index() or pass as_index=False.
  • Not noticing that rows with a missing group value are left out of the result by default.

6When should I use it?

Use groupby whenever the question contains the word per or by: sales per city, average per class, count per category.

7Interview questions

What does groupby() do?

It splits the data into groups by the values of one or more columns, applies a summary to each group and combines the results.

What does split-apply-combine mean?

The three steps behind groupby: split the rows into groups, apply a function to each group, combine the answers into one result.

How do you calculate several statistics per group?

Use agg, for example df.groupby("city")["amount"].agg(["sum", "mean"]).

How do you turn the group labels back into a column?

Call reset_index() on the result, or use as_index=False in groupby.
